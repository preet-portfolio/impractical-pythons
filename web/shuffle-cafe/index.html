                        How the board works

                        Two words are anagrams when they use the same letters, the same number of times. The trick is to put each word's letters in alphabetical order: listen and silent both become eilnst. Same sorted letters, same family.

                        The café does that sorting for all 60,388 words of a free dictionary once, when it opens, and files each word under its sorted letters. After that, finding your name's anagrams is a single look in the right drawer, all in Python, right here in your browser.

                        For phrases the café counts letters instead. Your name might have three es and one v; a word fits if it never needs more of any letter than the tray holds. Python picks out every word that fits, you pick the one that makes sense, and the count goes down. The computer is quick and the human has taste.

                        Shuffling is where computers stop being quick. Three letters can be ordered 6 ways, six letters 720 ways, ten letters over three million. The waiter lays out every arrangement when there are a few hundred, and a random handful when there are more.

                        The sieve uses a code-breaker's trick: every language leaves fingerprints. The café counts every pair and every run of three letters in its 60,388 words, and notes the pattern of consonants and vowels each word has. Orderings with a fingerprint English almost never leaves get caught. It isn't perfect: doughnut is caught, because hardly any word has hnu in it.

                        The chef searches like someone packing a suitcase: try the biggest word that fits, then the next, and back up when nothing fits the gap. It gives up after a few hundred thousand tries, so a long name never keeps you waiting, and ranks what it found so long words come first. Peek at the Python.
